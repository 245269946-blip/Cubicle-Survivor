# Art Direction

## Anchor

The visual target is hand-drawn neon micro-pixel, close to the provided Neon Abyss / illustrated room references:

- Hand-painted chunky silhouettes, not clean vector UI and not plain tile-grid pixel art.
- Thick dark-purple ink outlines around characters, props, cards, and important UI blocks.
- Dense readable prop storytelling: monitors, papers, cables, warning stickers, vending machines, office mess, tiny signs.
- Saturated neon accents on top of deep purple/teal shadows: cyan, magenta, violet, hot yellow, orange-red.
- Micro-pixel UI language: small block icons, bold outlined type, compact panels, arcade signage.
- Lighting feels like a stage or arcade cabinet: spotlights, colored rim lights, glowing signs, occasional confetti sparks.

## Avoid

- Empty square floor blocks or office-grid filler.
- Pure cyberpunk glass panels with no hand-drawn warmth.
- Single-hue blue/purple scenes without warm accent colors.
- Realistic office furniture without exaggeration.
- Clean flat icons that do not share the chunky outline language.

## Game-Specific Translation

- The office is not a normal office. It is a ridiculous overtime arcade stage.
- Every stage should read as a themed room diorama: desks, signs, cables, machines, posters, and task debris.
- Weapons and bullets should look like playful office props turned into neon toys.
- UI should feel like stickers, cabinet labels, receipts, and small arcade panels layered over the scene.
- Enemies are personified office events, not generic monsters: Bugs, requirement changes, meetings, deadlines, reviews, and alerts should read as paperwork, screens, warning lights, chat bubbles, and stressed office objects with faces.
